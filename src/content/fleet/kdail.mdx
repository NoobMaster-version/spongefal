## Mission

A naval dockyard needs autonomous forklifts that know where they are across a
**200 × 200 m** area — indoors and out, under cranes and steel, where GPS is
unreliable and wheel encoders drift with every slip. Target: **1–2 cm
localization accuracy.**

## Constraints → sensor choice

We evaluated Bluetooth, LoRaWAN, RFID, NFC, radar, CCTV, IR, and hyperspectral
options against the accuracy/range/infrastructure trade — and chose
**Ultra-Wideband**, on the Qorvo DWM3001CDK development kit.

## Build — scaling in stages

1. **Prototype:** Raspberry Pi 4B running ROS, a DWM3001CDK tag, a 9-DOF IMU
   for orientation, DC-motor base; SLAM fusing UWB and LIDAR
2. **2 × 2 m indoor** — proof the trilateration math worked
3. **7 × 7 m outdoor** — UWB + IMU only: no encoders, no LIDAR
4. **50 × 60 m at IIT Gandhinagar** — validated against a surveyor's
   **total station**, not our own estimates

## The firmware problem

Stock firmware capped ranging at **45 meters** — useless for a dockyard. So we
implemented our own **Double-Sided Two-Way Ranging (DS-TWR)** protocol and
tuned the physical layer ourselves: switching between **channel 5 and channel
9**, adjusting **preamble length and PAC size**, and tuning the ranging **time
delays** for each configuration.

| Configuration | Preamble | Range | Accuracy |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Short-range | 128 | ~10 m | ±2 cm |
| Mid-range | 256 | ~50 m | ±10 cm |
| Long-range | 512 | **315 m** | ±40 cm (50×50 m area) |

The trade is physics, not settings: longer preambles buy range and cost
update rate; the time-delay budget (`poll_TX_to_response_RX` and friends) has
to be re-derived for every profile.

## Telemetry

- ±2 cm accuracy in areas up to 10 × 10 m
- Stable communication at **315 m** after the firmware rewrite
- **0.4% error across a 10,000 m² operational area**, validated with a total
  station
- SLAM + NAV2 autonomous navigation running on top of the localization layer

## Debrief

Three things this internship burned in: sensor selection is a systems decision,
not a datasheet lookup; every 10× jump in scale exposes a failure mode the
smaller test hid; and results only count when an *independent* instrument — a
total station, not our own code — says they're real.
