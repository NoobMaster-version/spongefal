The problem: a naval dockyard wants autonomous forklifts. GPS doesn't work
under all that steel. Wheel encoders drift every time a wheel slips. The
forklift needs to know where it is, within a couple of centimeters, across a
200 × 200 m yard.

We looked at bluetooth, LoRa, RFID, radar, cameras — and landed on UWB:
radio beacons that measure distance by timing the signal flight. Put three
around the yard, do the math, you have a position.

The bot: a Raspberry Pi running ROS, a UWB tag, an IMU, LIDAR, SLAM and NAV2
on top.

## the firmware part

The stock firmware maxes out at 45 meters. A dockyard is 200. So we wrote
our own ranging protocol (double-sided two-way ranging) and tuned the radio
ourselves — switching between channel 5 and channel 9, adjusting preamble
length and PAC size, re-deriving the timing delays for every configuration.

| config | preamble | range | accuracy |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| short | 128 | ~10 m | ±2 cm |
| mid | 256 | ~50 m | ±10 cm |
| long | 512 | 315 m | ±40 cm |

Longer preamble, more range, slower updates. There's no free lunch, only a
trade — here, play with it:

<UwbTradeoff />

We tested in stages: a 2×2 m room, then a 7×7 m yard with no encoders and
no LIDAR, then 50×60 m at IIT Gandhinagar — where we checked our numbers
against a surveyor's total station instead of trusting our own code. Final
result: 0.4% error across 10,000 m².
