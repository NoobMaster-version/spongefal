## Mission

NishCorp Technologies is building an autonomous wheelchair that climbs stairs.
When I joined, the flagship design existed as a CAD model. My job: make it
exist in physics — prove the motion in simulation before the startup spent
money on hardware.

## Build

The core challenge was **ROS spawning and then implementing the same stack on
hardware** — getting the CAD model to exist correctly in simulation, then
proving the identical behaviour physically:

- Converted the provided CAD model to URDF and spawned it in Gazebo
- Applied kinematic models for differential-drive control; analyzed motion and
  steering behaviour
- Added a Gazebo IMU plugin and sensor/actuator definitions; visualized in RViz
- Wrote teleoperation scripts for driving the simulated chair
- Then crossed sim-to-real: 3D-printed custom parts and helped assemble a
  **half-scale physical prototype** that could sit and stand, contributing to
  integration, testing, and field evaluations

## Telemetry

The simulation and prototype work became evidence in the startup's funding
case — **NishCorp secured ₹13 lakh in grants** during this period.

## Debrief

Simulation isn't a rehearsal — it's an economic instrument. Proving motion in
Gazebo cost the startup weeks; proving it in steel would have cost lakhs. The
sim-to-real discipline I practiced here (model it, verify it, then build it at
reduced scale) is now how I approach every hardware project.
