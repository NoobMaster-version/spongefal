Every robot I've built since traces back to these three. Together they cover the
three problems every mobile robot has: **feel** (reading the world through sensors),
**balance** (acting faster than physics), and **decide** (choosing a path).

## Chapter 1 — Feel: Gesture-Controlled Car

**Winter internship (SRW), first semester — my first real project.** Tilt your
hand and the car follows. An MPU6050 on the glove reads hand orientation, an
ESP32 streams it over ESP-NOW to a second ESP32 driving the motors through an
L298N, on a custom PCB.

<VideoEvidence
  src="origins/gesture-car.mp4"

The hard parts at the time: filtering raw IMU noise into stable gesture
detection, keeping wireless latency low enough that the car felt connected to
your hand, and writing code efficient enough to live on a battery.

## Chapter 2 — Balance: Wall-E, the Self-Balancing Robot

An inverted pendulum on two wheels: read the MPU6050, fuse gyro + accelerometer
through a **complementary filter**, run PID hundreds of times per second, drive
the motors before the robot falls over. Tuning it teaches you what P, I, and D
each *feel* like — the overshoot, the sag, the jitter.

<VideoEvidence
  src="origins/wall-e.mp4"
  poster="origins/wall-e-poster.jpg"
  caption="Wall-E holding balance on two wheels"
/>

Wall-E is more than my build: it's the **SRA workshop platform**. We manufacture,
assemble, and sell Wall-E kits, and have taught **more than 200 students**
embedded C, ESP32, MPU6050 integration, and complementary-filter math on them.
I went from building one to teaching with it.

## Chapter 3 — Decide: Line-Following Maze Solver

Built for the SRA maze competition — **4th place**. ESP32 on ESP-IDF, an IR
sensor array, a 100 Hz control loop. It follows the line with PID, maps the maze
with the left-hand rule while remembering every junction, then replays the
shortest path it found.

## Debrief

These three taught me the full loop — sense, filter, control, act — before I ever
touched ROS. The instinct that firmware should be honest about physics (latency,
noise, momentum) started here.
