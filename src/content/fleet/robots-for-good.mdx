Not everything needs SLAM. These three exist because someone — or some cat —
needed them.

## The Automatic Pet Feeder

Our robotics club has a cat. The cat does not observe weekends or semester
breaks. So: an SRA-Board-driven feeder with a servo-rotated dispenser and a
3D-printed chute, timer-scheduled so she eats even when the lab is empty.

## The River-Cleaning Boat

Built for my Environmental Studies course: a remote-controlled boat with a
front-mounted collection mechanism and onboard storage for floating debris, so
an operator can clean from the shore instead of wading in. Everything —
waterproof hull, propulsion, collection gate — built from scratch on a student
budget.

## The Coin Sorter (24-hour hackathon)

A machine that recognizes Indian coin denominations (₹1, ₹2, ₹5, ₹10) with a
camera and a CNN (transfer learning, running on a Raspberry Pi 4B), then routes
each coin to its container through servo-controlled gates — designed, trained,
built, and demoed **within 24 hours**.

<VideoEvidence
  src="robots-for-good/coin-demo.mp4"
  poster="robots-for-good/coin-demo-poster.jpg"
  caption="End-to-end: recognize, gate, sort"
/>

**Measured:** recognition accuracy above **95% for ₹5 and ₹10 coins**, with all
four denominations sorted end-to-end in the final demo.

## Debrief

Small robots with real users are honest teachers: nobody cares about your
architecture if the cat doesn't get fed. Reliability, not novelty, is the
deliverable.
