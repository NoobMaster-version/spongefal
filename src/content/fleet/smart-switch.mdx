## Mission

This started as a hobby project — and then I decided it should be a **proper
product**, not a demo. Retrofit ordinary electrical switches so they can be
controlled from a phone anywhere, *without* breaking the physical switches
people already trust. That one requirement (wall switch and app must always
agree) drives most of the engineering below.

## Constraints

- **220 V AC** mains on one side, 3.3 V logic on the other — safety isn't optional
- Wall switches must keep working with the cloud down
- WiFi credentials must be configurable by a normal person (no re-flashing)

## Build

Five layers, built end to end:

1. **Mechanical** — CAD-designed switch box and buttons
2. **Electronics** — custom perf board: relays, MOSFETs, and optocouplers
   isolating the ESP32 from 220 V AC
3. **Firmware (ESP-IDF / FreeRTOS)** — manages 6 devices with GPIO mapping and
   debounced interrupts; **BLE GATT provisioning** (phone sends
   `{"ssid": "...", "password": "..."}` — no hardcoded WiFi); state persisted
   in NVS across power cuts; a heartbeat JSON published every 5 seconds
4. **Cloud** — MQTT broker with TLS 1.2, QoS 1 and Last-Will messages; topic
   scheme `smart_switch/<board_id>/<device>/command` with ON/OFF/TOGGLE/STATUS
5. **App (Flutter)** — control, rename, and live state for every switch.
   Honestly: the app was vibe-coded — fast, focused on being usable. The
   firmware was not.

## Engineering challenges

- **High-voltage safety:** optocoupled relay stage between mains and logic
- **State synchronization:** wall switch, firmware state, cloud, and app must
  never disagree — solved with retained state, NVS persistence, and the
  heartbeat
- **Provisioning UX:** BLE onboarding instead of hardcoded credentials
- **Debouncing** real, aging wall switches at the interrupt level

## Debrief

Full-stack means something different in hardware: physics to pixels. The hard
part of IoT wasn't connectivity — it was **state consistency** across four
places a switch's truth can live. Next steps: move the perf board to a proper
KiCAD PCB, OTA updates, then public release of the firmware.
